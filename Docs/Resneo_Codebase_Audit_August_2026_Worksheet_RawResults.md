### D1-availability-engine

**Architecture summary (finder agent):**

The appointment availability domain has three layers, and the core rules layer is genuinely single-sourced. (1) PURE RULES: `appointment-engine.ts` (1,860 lines) holds three entry points sharing one rule set: `computeAppointmentAvailability` (slot generation), `validateExactAppointmentStart` (exact-time write gate, fixed-times still enforced), and `validateAppointmentCustomInterval` (staff move/resize/walk-in with per-gate overrides: allowOutsideHours, allowDuringBreaks, allowInsideNoticeWindow, allowBookingOverlap; full-day leave is checked ahead of every override, per SA-M3). All arithmetic is venue-local wall-clock minutes on half-open intervals; the only UTC conversions live in `venue-local-clock.ts`, whose two-pass offset resolver is DST-correct and test-pinned (SA-H1's broken sibling was deleted, not deprecated). Minimum notice is deliberately wall-clock (`slotMinutesFromNow`) so it is stable across DST. Hours resolve through two shared modules: `calendar-hours.ts` (calendar weekly hours, per-date overrides that REPLACE, breaks; explicitly split into anchor-vs-veto semantics) and `venue-wide-business-hours.ts` (the shared venue resolver: weekly baseline in three states, amended-hours REPLACE with most-specific-wins, closures materialised and subtracted last). Crucially, closures and breaks are VETOED per candidate rather than subtracted from anchoring ranges, so a part-day closure never re-anchors the afternoon grid; candidate starts come from `booking-interval.ts` (interval grid anchored to range start, hour-anchored minute marks, or fixed times). Buffer/processing time comes from `processing-time.ts`: busy intervals with gaps (`practitionerBusyMinuteOffsets`), and `peakConcurrentBookings` counts true peak concurrency against `parallel_clients`. Boundary behaviour at day edges, buffers and variants is correct in everything I traced. Notably, the prior scheduling audit's "two resolvers" problem (SA-H2 etc.) has substantially SHIPPED as the Resolver Plan Stages 0-7: the parity harness (`parity/scheduling-world.ts` + 6 test files) drives one fixture through every engine, asserts ordered start lists, and marks each old divergence CONVERGED; the one still-pinned divergence is classes/events listing on a weekly-closed weekday vs the create gate (parity-matrix.test.ts:98). (2) FETCHERS: this is where discipline decays. There are FOUR near-copy fetchers: day legacy (`fetchAppointmentInput`), day unified (`fetchCalendarAppointmentInput`), month legacy and month unified factories in `appointment-month-availability.ts` (1,029 lines), plus a fifth in `appointment-catalog.ts`. Every production venue is unified (SA-M13 verified zero `practitioners` rows), so the legacy halves are dead weight. Booking rows map through one shared `mapRowToAppointmentBooking` (a past drift, deliberately unified), but the month unified factory has drifted from the day one in custom-duration and processing-block handling. Reads fail open (`res.data ?? []`) but are instrumented via `availability-read-failure.ts` -> AsyncLocalStorage collector (`schedule-read-context.ts`) -> `withScheduleFailClosed`, which converts a known-incomplete 200 into a 503 on guest routes (Stage 7); this works only for reads that call the reporter, and a handful do not. Model-owned blocks (scheduled classes/events via `calendar-session-blocks.ts`, resource windows) fold into `practitionerBlockedRanges`, gated per booking model by `blocked-range-models.ts` (fails closed to all-blocking). Leave: full-day folds into `days_off` AND rides `fullDayLeavePractitionerIds` (override-proof); partial leave becomes blocked ranges of kind `leave`. (3) API: `/api/booking/availability` is the live guest day route (variant/add-on/custom-duration aware, any-available pooling via `appointment-any-practitioner.ts`); `/api/booking/appointment-calendar` is the month picker (per-date engine evaluation off month-scoped fetches); `/api/booking/validate-appointment-slot` is the exact-start gate (zod-validated, collective-aware); `/api/booking/unified-availability` is a second, repo-consumerless public day route wrapping `getUnifiedAvailableSlots`. Enforcement layering: date-level booking window at routes (`isGuestBookingDateAllowed`, applied on month and unified-availability, absent on the live day route, SA-M8), per-slot min-notice inside the engine, and create-time re-validation via `revalidate-appointment-slot`/`validateExactAppointmentStart`. Tests are strong: behavioural, regression-anchored (each pins a named production bug), injected clocks, and a Supabase fake that actually applies filters; the parity harness is the standout.

**Quality assessment (finder agent):**

The rules core is excellent: one implementation each of hours, breaks, closures, intervals and processing time, correct half-open boundary handling, deliberate and documented anchor-vs-veto semantics, DST handled once and correctly, and an unusually good parity harness that pins divergences as explicit expectations. Comments consistently record why, cite audit IDs, and name the bug each rule fixed; tests pin behaviour rather than mirroring implementation. Judged as an engine, this is well above production average.

The fetch layer is where the discipline runs out. Four near-copy input builders (day/month x legacy/unified) plus a fifth catalog builder total roughly 1,300 lines where two would do, and the copies have already drifted (month vs day custom-duration and processing-block handling; SA-M10 still open). The legacy halves are dead in production by the project's own verified query. A second, consumerless public day-slot route duplicates the live one with different gating and roughly double the queries. Small utilities are also duplicated (three interval-intersections, two union-merges, two getDayOfWeek exports, a "single source of truth" status list with three local copies and one genuinely drifted consumer). The SA-C3 fail-open instrumentation is near-complete but not complete: three scheduled-session reads and one service_items read evade both Sentry and the Stage 7 fail-closed wrapper. Net: sound and safe at the rules layer, over-built and repetitive at the fetch layer; the right work is deletion and consolidation, not redesign.

#### AE-8 [inconsistency/low] Cross-midnight duration gets three different answers across validator, row mapper and modify-end resolver
- Anchor: `src/lib/availability/appointment-engine.ts:237`
- Detail: `minutesBetweenStartAndEnd` (:234-241) silently wraps end<=start by +24h, so validateAppointmentCustomInterval accepts a 23:30->00:30 staff resize as a 60-minute booking whose busyEnd (1470) extends past minute 1440, where no next-day breaks, blocks or bookings are loaded (the engine is single-date). - Suggested fix: Make validateAppointmentCustomInterval refuse end<=start explicitly ('Appointments cannot cross midnight'), matching the SA-M27 structural rule for hours windows, and clamp resolveAppointmentModifyEndCoreHHmm to <24:00. Delete the wrap in minutesBetweenStartAndEnd.
- **Verdict: CONFIRMED** - All three semantics verified: minutesBetweenStartAndEnd wraps +24h (:234-241) and feeds validateAppointmentCustomInterval at :905; mapRowToAppointmentBooking deliberately refuses the wrap with the stale-row rationale (:1113-1128); resolveAppointmentModifyEndCoreHHmm uses minutesToTime with no modulo (index.ts:38-41 has none), so 23:00+90min emits '24:30'. The normal-hours gates DO refuse a past-midnight busyEnd (fitsInRange :990), so exposure requires allowOutsideHours, which the finder's scenario already stipulates. One partial rescuing layer on the resolver limb only: Postgres time columns reject '24:30:00', so that limb fails the write loudly (500) rather than persisting; the wrap limb ('23:30'->'00:30', both valid time values) persists and stands exactly as described — spill invisible to the next day and the row thereafter modelled at catalogue duration. ### D2-public-booking-flow

**Architecture summary (finder agent):**

The public/guest appointment booking flow spans three layers.

CLIENT — `src/components/booking/AppointmentBookingFlow.tsx` (5,903 lines; 48 useState, 20 useEffect, 53 useMemo/useCallback, ~30 `Step` values). It is a hand-rolled step machine. `src/lib/booking/appointment-flow-order.ts` (214 lines) was extracted to hold the ordering logic (service-first vs staff-first, venue/combined/locked surfaces) as pure, tested transition functions; the rest of the machine — state, data fetching, and per-step JSX — stays in the component. It fetches a catalog (`/api/booking/appointment-catalog`), month availability (`/api/booking/appointment-calendar`, cached in a Map + in-flight ref with a 4-worker prefetch pool), and day slots (`/api/booking/availability`). It builds `MultiServiceSegment[]` for visits and `PersonSelection[]` for groups, folding variant + add-on duration/price into each. Charge previews use `resolveAppointmentServiceOnlineCharge(WithAddons)` from `src/lib/appointments/appointment-service-payment.ts`. The same component also serves staff (audience prop) and edit mode. `UnifiedBookingForm.tsx` (1,903 lines) is the staff TABLE-reservation form and is only tangentially related (posts to `/api/venue/bookings`).

SERVER — three create routes:
- `/api/booking/create` (2,186 lines): rate-limited (8/min), Zod-validated. Dispatches by `resolveVenueMode` + `inferSecondaryBookingModelFromPayload`. The `POST` body handles the table (Model A) branch inline; `handleNonTableBooking` handles unified_scheduling (appointment + event_session group session), event_ticket, class_session, resource_booking. Every branch RE-fetches the engine input, RE-validates the slot, and RE-derives price/deposit/card-hold from the catalog — the client's amounts are never trusted. `booking_end_time`/`estimated_end_time` are derived by `bookingEndFieldsForStorage`. Combined-collective bookings are resolved server-side via `isCollectiveId`/`resolveCombinedBookingTarget`, rewriting venue_id + service and applying a price/duration override.
- `/api/booking/create-multi-service` (1,057 lines) and `/api/booking/create-group` (1,034 lines): one guest, one practitioner, consecutive services vs many people. NOT rate-limited. Both also serve the staff mobile app (source phone/walk-in), so they carry `resolveStaffVisitChargeDiscretion` (request deposit/card-hold flags honoured only for staff sources, ignored for public) and the `isStaffWalkInBookingDateAllowed` split.
- `/api/booking/validate-appointment-slot` (247 lines): per-segment exact-start re-check the client calls while assembling a visit chain.

ENFORCEMENT LAYERS: slot availability is `computeAppointmentAvailability` / `validateExactAppointmentStart` (`appointment-engine.ts`); booking-window (advance/min-notice/same-day) is `isGuestBookingDateAllowed` (`entity-booking-window.ts`); venue-wide hours/closures are the `*RejectBookingWindow` gates. The appointment race is narrowed (not closed) by `createAppointmentSlotRecheck` (`revalidate-appointment-slot.ts`) re-run immediately before insert. The database layer is `enforce_cde_capacity` (migration 20261225120000): an advisory-lock-serialised BEFORE INSERT/UPDATE trigger that guards capacity/overlap for `experience_event_id`, `class_instance_id`, and `resource_id` ONLY — appointments and event_sessions are explicitly not governed. Stripe: `resolveCaptureMode` picks payment / setup (card hold) / payment_with_setup; PaymentIntent/SetupIntent creation, the load-bearing linkage write, and cancel-on-failure cleanup are repeated in each route. Compliance is gated per booking/segment via `checkBookingCompliance`/`enforceBookingCompliance` (fail-open). Confirmation email/SMS is fired via `after()` only on no-payment bookings; paid bookings notify from the payment-confirm path.

**Quality assessment (finder agent):**

The server discipline is genuinely good on the point that matters most: every create route re-fetches the engine input and re-derives price, duration, deposit, card-hold fee, ticket lines and slot validity from the catalog, so a crafted request cannot book at a price or length the UI would forbid (the `booking_end_time` rejection comment at create/route.ts:1775 shows this was learned the hard way). The known appointment double-booking race (C3/SA-C1) is honestly documented and interim-mitigated.

The weak points are two. First, breadth of enforcement is uneven across the six models: the CDE capacity trigger and the slot-recheck both skip `event_session_id` (unified group sessions), leaving that model's capacity on a pure read-then-insert with a ~900-line gap and no past/notice/window guard — the one place the "multiple enforcing layers" principle genuinely has a hole. Second, the code is heavily over-built by duplication rather than over-engineered by abstraction: `create-multi-service` and `create-group` are ~85% the same 1,000 lines; the single create route repeats its entire deposit/card-hold/PaymentIntent/confirmation-email machinery twice; and the 5,900-line client component copy-pastes its single-flow steps into `group_*` steps (the add-on step logic appears twice verbatim). None of this is wrong, but it is a large, growing maintenance surface where the four Stripe branches and three add-on validators must be kept in lockstep by hand. Client-vs-server amount display has one small drift (single-service full-payment add-on under-quote). Rate limiting is present on `create` but absent on the two visit routes and the waitlist join. Overall: sensible and safe on the write path, but under-abstracted and with one under-defended model.

### D3-staff-appointments

**Architecture summary (finder agent):**

Staff-side appointment management is three surfaces over one API family. The surfaces: PractitionerCalendarView.tsx (8,903 lines; the exported component alone spans lines 2483-8903, ~6,420 lines) renders the day/week/month diary, drag-move, resize, undo, quick status actions, block CRUD, linked-venue columns and four detail sheets; DaySheetView.tsx (1,836) is a per-day service list with its own /api/venue/day-sheet endpoint, realtime channel and polling fallback; BookingsDashboard/AppointmentBookingsDashboard (2,618/2,315) are the list registries, splitting at page.tsx on venue model, with ExpandedBookingContent (2,417) as the shared inline row body and BookingDetailPanel as the calendar popover. All three fetch /api/venue/bookings/list, subscribe to Supabase postgres_changes on `bookings` and converge by debounced refetch; optimistic writes use snapshot-rollback plus a booking-row overlay store pruned against fresh rows (applyBookingsList, PractitionerCalendarView:3304-3317). A module-level group-visit cache (group-visit-bookings.ts) is primed from every list response and shared across surfaces.

The API: POST /api/venue/bookings (2,046 lines) is one handler with five create branches (event/class/resource/appointment/table) dispatched by which id is present; the appointment branch runs the same engine as public create (fetchAppointmentInput + computeAppointmentAvailability or validateAppointmentCustomInterval, plus a C3-interim slot recheck), with walk-ins deliberately bypassing overlap/hours/notice gates but still checked against leave/classes (route.ts:1157-1193). PATCH /api/venue/bookings/[id] (482-3033) is the everything-mutation endpoint: ~8 operation families and 14+ leaf operations dispatched by sniffing body keys (attendance-only fast path, processing-blocks-only, status with Cancelled/No-Show/general sub-branches, client_arrived, attendance-general, notes+guest identity, then schedule modify sub-branched by inferBookingRowModel into resource/class/event-reject/appointment/table). Appointment reschedules all funnel through validateAppointmentModificationInterval (lib/booking/validate-appointment-modification.ts), the SAME engine path as public availability, with three explicit staff overrides (allow_manual_overlap, allow_outside_hours, allow_during_breaks) mirrored by the dry-run route /[id]/validate-appointment-modification. Optimistic concurrency is `.eq('updated_at', prev)` -> 412; resource/class capacity is additionally backstopped by the enforce_cde_capacity DB trigger (23P01 -> 409). Cancellation deadlines are re-pinned per entity via resolveRescheduleCancellationDeadline (C13), comm logs reset on start change, guest notify runs in after() with defer/skip flags the calendar uses for its 60-second undo pill.

Multi-service visits are N bookings rows sharing group_booking_id, made first-class by lib/booking/appointment-visit.ts (resolveAppointmentVisit/resequenceVisit/distributeVisitDuration, buffer-vs-orphaned-gap distinction) and two visit endpoints (/api/venue/visits/[gid]/schedule and /services) that plan, dry-run, write row-by-row with server-side rollback and one guest notification. The modify form uses those endpoints; the calendar instead re-implements visit move (patchVisitMove, per-row dry-run then per-row PATCHes) and visit resize (patchVisitResize, no dry-run at all) client-side. Group semantics are guarded by one rule in group-booking-status-sync.ts: cascade only when every sibling row has no person_label and no class_instance_id (the C12/H8 fix), consulted by resolveCascadingVisitGroupId in every [id] status branch and re-applied verbatim in staff-cancel-booking.ts.

Cancellation exists twice: the [id] PATCH Cancelled branch (911-1224: shared-PI proportional refund via planSharedDepositRefund, refund-failure aborts cancel, settleCardHoldsOnCancellation keeps late-cancel holds chargeable, per-segment waitlist offers) and cancelStaffBookingWithNotify in staff-cancel-booking.ts (serving class-instance and event cascade cancels; always releases holds, deliberately; restores class credits/membership; applies lifecycle effects; computes the per-entity refund-window for its guest copy). The divergences between these copies are where the defects live.

Conflict enforcement layering, as verified: client preview (appointmentWindowCollides et al., advisory only), server engine validation on every schedule-changing PATCH (with staff overrides; every calendar gesture sends allow_manual_overlap:true per SA-L4), engine recheck before create insert, DB triggers for CDE capacity only, nothing at DB level for plain appointments (C3, known). No staff modification path skips server validation outright; the weaknesses are atomicity (client-side visit orchestration) and the ever-on overlap override. AppointmentAvailabilitySettings and the availability editors were covered by the prior scheduling audit and re-confirmed only incidentally here.

**Quality assessment (finder agent):**

This domain is far better than its file sizes suggest. The server validation architecture is genuinely disciplined: one shared interval validator used by dry-run, single PATCH and both visit endpoints; consistent staff-override flags; optimistic-concurrency guards; deadline/notify/comm-reset handled uniformly; and the code carries unusually honest comments citing prior audit IDs (C8, C9, C13, SA-H5, SA-M3), meaning past findings were internalised, not patched over. Client state discipline (snapshot rollback, overlay pruning, debounced realtime) is above average.

"Today"/now-line timezone handling is solved correctly in one surface and left UTC/browser-local in the other two. The pattern across findings is consistent: the primitives are right, but duplicated orchestration layers drift; consolidation, not redesign, is what this codebase needs.

### D4-payments

**Architecture summary (finder agent):**

**Money model.** All amounts are integer pence end-to-end (`deposit_amount_pence`, `fee_pence`, `amount_pence`). Floats exist only at boundaries: availability engines expose `slot.deposit_amount` in GBP and create routes convert with `Math.round(x*100)` (src/app/api/booking/create/route.ts:457,469); display formats with `/100`. There is no float arithmetic on stored money.

**Deposit lifecycle (money mode).** A create route computes the charge via `resolveAppointmentServiceOnlineCharge` (src/lib/appointments/appointment-service-payment.ts, 117 lines, pure) and inserts the booking `Pending/Pending`, then creates one PaymentIntent per *capture unit* (a booking, a group/visit, or a class cart; every row carries the same `stripe_payment_intent_id`). Confirmation is triple-redundant: (1) the client calls `POST /api/booking/confirm-payment` (327 lines), which re-verifies the intent with Stripe; (2) the webhook (`src/app/api/webhooks/stripe/route.ts`, 931 lines) is the backup; (3) two self-heal layers (`selfHealSucceededPaymentIntent`, invoked by the auto-cancel sweeps and the daily reconciliation cron) repair paid-but-Pending units. All three funnel into one DB-only helper, `confirmBookingsForSucceededPaymentIntent` / `...SetupIntent` (src/lib/booking/confirm-deposit-payment.ts, 606 lines), whose writes are conditional (`eq status Pending`, `in deposit_status [Pending,Failed]`) so replays are no-ops and a zero-row flip is classified (replay vs "abandonment sweep cancelled the unit" — the J2 race) by re-reading statuses. Webhook idempotency is a claim table (`webhook_events`) with completed_at stamping, stale-claim reclaim after 15 min, and release-on-error (src/lib/webhooks/stripe-event-idempotency.ts).

**Card holds.** `booking_card_holds` is 1:1 with bookings; three capture modes (`payment`/`setup`/`payment_with_setup`, src/lib/booking/card-hold-capture.ts). The hold snapshots the connected account, a booking-scoped Stripe customer, and a versioned consent text (card-hold-terms.ts). The no-show charge engine (card-hold-charge.ts, 881 lines) implements a CAS claim (`charge_attempt_count` + `charge_payment_intent_id IS NULL`), attempt-numbered idempotency keys, conditional PI-id persist, and orphan-PI disposal (cancel, else refund) — genuinely race-hardened. Release (card-hold-release.ts) stamps `released_at`+reason, guards charged holds, and best-effort deletes the Stripe customer with a shared-customer sibling check. Cancellation settlement (card-hold-cancellation.ts) keeps saved holds chargeable after a late cancel, but only under consent version ≥2.

**Refunds.** Since the C11 fix, `planSharedDepositRefund` (shared-deposit-refund.ts) computes a per-member share of a shared PI with a set-keyed idempotency key; amount-less full refunds are kept when the settle covers every still-Paid row. Four call sites repeat the plan→refund→stamp block: guest cancel (src/app/api/confirm/route.ts:591-675), staff cancel helper (src/lib/booking/staff-cancel-booking.ts:158-250), venue PATCH cancel (src/app/api/venue/bookings/[id]/route.ts:975-1050), staff deposit route (src/app/api/venue/bookings/[id]/deposit/route.ts:416-447). The `charge.refunded` webhook only stamps siblings on a FULL charge refund and skips hold-bearing and ledger rows.

**In-person balances.** A separate `booking_payments` ledger (charge route, 610 lines) with client-minted attempt-id idempotency; the webhook is the source of truth (`confirm-balance-payment.ts`), and `recomputeBookingPaymentSummary` (payment-summary.ts, 701 lines) derives denormalised per-row `amount_paid_pence` and visit-scoped `payment_state` live from deposits + ledger.

**Crons** (vercel.json): `auto-cancel-bookings` (*/10, 842 lines, five sweeps: phone deposits 24h, staff holds 24h, online setup-mode 20m, online money 20m, no-PI 20m — each verifies the intent with Stripe first, self-heals succeeded ones, only acts on rows its conditional UPDATE actually flipped, then kills the PI); `deposit-reminder-2h` (*/30, comm-log deduped); `release-card-holds` (daily, derived 14-day charge window, 200-row batch); `reconciliation` (daily: Paid/Refunded vs Stripe, card-hold arms, stuck-Pending self-heal); `subscription-reconcile` (platform billing backstop). The `/api/confirm` route itself (2,014 lines) is not the payment confirm: it is the guest manage-link GET/confirm/cancel/modify handler, whose cancel leg owns the guest-side refund and hold settlement, and whose modify leg contains four model-specific reschedule branches.

The code matches Docs/CARD_HOLD_DEPOSITS_DESIGN_AND_IMPLEMENTATION.md closely; section references in code are accurate where checked (§7.4, §8.3, §9.2, §12.1-12.4).

**Quality assessment (finder agent):**

This is the strongest domain I have seen in this codebase. The payments core is genuinely well-disciplined: integer-pence arithmetic throughout, conditional-UPDATE idempotency at every state flip, a real webhook claim table with stale reclaim, deterministic Stripe idempotency keys everywhere money moves, snapshotted connected accounts on holds so account changes cannot orphan refunds, and defence-in-depth (client confirm → webhook → two self-heal sweeps → daily reconciliation) that closes almost every charged-without-booking / booked-without-charge window. Comments cite the design doc and prior review findings, and the doc matches the code. The card-hold charge engine's CAS claim + orphan-PI disposal is unusually careful.

Where it is under-built: the *edges* of the refund model. The C11 partial-refund change was landed in the four settle paths but not propagated to the reconciliation cron (false alerts, and a real unhealed partial-refund state), and two of the four copy-pasted settle blocks lack the already-refunded convergence the other two have — the cost of not extracting one helper. `record_cash` kills the deposit PI but leaves its id on a 'Paid' row, hard-blocking later cancels. Crons trust small datasets: unbounded or 200-capped serial loops with no maxDuration and no alert dedupe.

Where it is over-built: only mildly — `/api/confirm` interleaves seven flows in 2,014 lines with a 4× repeated modify scaffold, and one unit-guessing money heuristic survives in the comms layer. Nothing here is gratuitous complexity; most length is earned by real race handling.

### D5-classes

**Architecture summary (finder agent):**

The class domain has four layers.

**Availability**: `src/lib/availability/class-session-engine.ts` (593 lines) is the single engine. `fetchClassInput` / `fetchClassInputForRange` (near-identical twins) load class_types, class_instances, venue row, venue-wide blocks and capacity-consuming bookings; `computeClassAvailability` filters cancelled/inactive rows, applies venue closure overlap (explicit closures only, not weekly hours: a class is a fixed staff-placed time), per-class-type guest booking windows (min notice, window days, via `entityBookingWindowFromRow` in venue-local wall time; DST-safe), and resolves `payment_requirement` (none/deposit/full_payment/card_hold with flag-gated degrade). Capacity = capacity_override ?? class_type.capacity minus summed party_size over `CAPACITY_CONSUMING_STATUSES` ('Booked','Confirmed','Pending','Seated') which exactly matches the DB trigger. Public APIs (`/api/booking/class-offerings`, `class-instances`) run this engine, fail closed, and are gated on venue model exposure and light-plan blocks.

**Booking writes**: `insert-free-class-session-booking.ts` (280 lines, status Booked) and `insert-pending-paid-class-session-booking.ts` (229 lines, status Pending + deposit_amount_pence) each re-fetch the instance, class type, re-run the engine as a friendly pre-check, compute cancellation deadline and end-time fields, then insert. The authoritative race guard is the DB trigger `enforce_cde_capacity` (migration 20261225120000): advisory lock per class_instance_id, re-counts party_size, raises 23P01/'CDE_CAPACITY' which every caller maps to 409. This is the layer that makes the app-level pre-checks safe.

**Commerce** (`src/lib/class-commerce/`, ~4.7k lines): `quoteClassCart` prices each line from the engine slot (deposit/full × party size), applies the best membership `discount_percent`, and resolves card-hold fees. `orchestrateClassCartCheckout` re-quotes server-side (quote and checkout share one price computation; the discounted amount is passed to the paid insert as a capped override), then per line resolves entitlement in `decideClassLineEntitlement` precedence free → course → membership → credits(opt-in) → card. Course coverage = active enrollment linked to the instance (`class_course_session_enrollments`); membership coverage = `membershipCoversClassType` (unlimited, else highest-remaining allowance computed from a period-windowed ledger sum with rollover carry-over); credits = `consume_class_credits_atomically` Postgres RPC (advisory lock per user+venue, FIFO by expiry, class-type eligibility, idempotent per booking). Paid lines share one PaymentIntent (optionally vaulting the card for card-hold lines); free/covered lines are Booked immediately with deferred confirmations; any failure triggers a compensating rollback (restore ledgers, delete rows, delete Stripe customer). `persistClassCartCheckoutTransaction` writes an audit row + per-booking allocations, idempotent on PI id.

**Fulfilment & lifecycle**: The Stripe webhook is the source of truth: PI purposes route to `fulfill-credit-purchase` (unique fulfillment-lock row + compensation), `fulfill-course-enrollment` (conditional status flip + session linking), the shared `confirmBookingsForSucceededPaymentIntent` for cart Pending→Booked, and `syncClassMembershipFromStripeSubscription` (upsert keyed on unique stripe_subscription_id, lifecycle comms). Cancel/refund is multi-path: guest self-cancel (`/api/confirm`) restores credits/allowance only inside the cancellation deadline; venue-initiated cascades (`cancelClassInstanceBookings` → `cancelStaffBookingWithNotify`) restore unconditionally with shared-PI proportional refunds (C11/C12 fixes applied via `isCascadingVisitGroup`); the webhook restores + frees seats on refunds/payment failures. Restores are ledger-append reversals keyed per redeem row (`restore-class-credits`, `restore-membership-allowance`), detected via `booking-was-credit-paid`.

**Crons** (all under `/api/cron/`): `class-recurring-materialize` walks due `class_recurring_reservations` (zod-validated weekday/time/interval rules, membership `allow_recurring` re-checked each run) and books free classes 28 days ahead, idempotent per guest+instance; date math is UTC-noon based and DST-safe. `class-membership-period-reset` writes one `period_reset` carry-over row per period (idempotent key membership+period_start). `class-credit-expiry` writes `expire` ledger rows and zeroes batches. `reconcile-class-memberships` re-fetches every live subscription from Stripe as the webhook backstop. `expire-pending-course-enrollments` releases seats held by abandoned paid checkouts with claim-then-cancel-PI and a revive path for late payments.

**Dashboard**: `ClassTimetableView.tsx` (1700 lines) is timetable CRUD over `/api/venue/classes` + `class-instances` (bulk insert idempotent via unique (class_type_id,instance_date,start_time)); `ClassCommerceProductsClient.tsx` (1853 lines) is three product CRUD panels; all staff class-commerce routes gate through `requireClassCommercePlan` (tier + model + feature flag).

**Quality assessment (finder agent):**

This is a substantially better-engineered domain than its size suggests. The hard problems are handled in the right layer: capacity is enforced by a DB advisory-lock trigger, credit spend by an atomic locked RPC, purchase fulfilment by unique-key locks with compensation, and course-seat expiry by claim-then-act with a revive path. Quote and checkout share one price computation, the entitlement precedence is a single pure function matching the product doc, and recurring date math is genuinely DST-safe. Crons are mostly idempotent and re-runnable.

The weaknesses cluster in exactly two places. First, the reverse paths never received the discipline the forward paths did: restore of credits/allowance is non-atomic JS read-modify-write while consume is a locked RPC; membership allowance consume has no lock at all; one of the three staff cancel surfaces (the main dashboard PATCH) restores nothing; the expiry cron silently claws back restored credits. Symmetry between consume and restore was asserted in comments but not built. Second, `materialize-recurring-reservation` is 454 lines of untested date logic with two defects (fortnightly phase stall, horizon-boundary miss) that make the feature quietly unreliable.

Over-building is modest and localized: the two class insert helpers and the two engine fetchers are near-copies (~350 avoidable lines), the checkout runs the full availability engine ~2N+ times per cart, and the course proration machinery is unreachable dead weight given the cancellation-window gate. The dashboard clients are large monoliths but internally coherent. Net: sensible architecture, strong forward-path discipline, with the money-return half a clear tier below the money-take half.

### D6-events

**Architecture summary (finder agent):**

The ticketed-events domain ("experience events") is a materialised-occurrence model. Every occurrence is its own `experience_events` row (venue_id, event_date, start/end time, capacity, per-event booking-window columns, payment_requirement, deposit_amount_pence) with child `event_ticket_types` tiers. Recurrence is materialised at CREATE time, not by cron: POST /api/venue/experience-events (779 lines) expands a weekly/custom schedule via `expandWeeklyOccurrences`/`normaliseCustomDates` (src/lib/scheduling/experience-event-dates.ts, cap 104 from cde-scheduling-rules.ts) into N rows, designating the first as series parent and pointing siblings' `parent_event_id` at it (CDE review C8). There is NO series-level edit or re-materialisation; PATCH edits one occurrence.

Availability: src/lib/availability/event-ticket-engine.ts (486 lines) is compute + fetch. `computeEventAvailability` filters inactive events, applies venue-wide closures/amended-hours via the shared resolver (`resolveVenueWideAllowedMinuteRanges`, decision H: weekly-closed days still sell), applies guest booking windows (`entityBookingWindowFromRow` + min-notice, venue-timezone aware) when guestCtx is passed, and computes remaining = capacity − Σ party_size of capacity-consuming bookings, per-tier remaining = min(event remaining, tier cap − Σ booking_ticket_lines quantities). `buildEventOfferingSummaries` groups occurrences by series_key (parent_event_id ?? id) for the catalogue. Consumed by public GET /api/booking/event-offerings (365-day scan, fail-closed wrapper) and staff GET /api/venue/event-offerings (no guestCtx).

Booking enforcement is 3-layered: (1) client picks from engine `instances`; (2) both write routes (public /api/booking/create ~2185 lines, staff /api/venue/bookings ~2046 lines) recompute the engine slot and run the shared `validateEventTicketBooking` (tier ownership, per-tier remaining, party_size == Σ qty, server-derived prices/deposit/card-hold fee — the M1/C2 fix); (3) DB trigger `enforce_cde_capacity` (20261225120000) takes a per-event advisory lock and re-counts Σ party_size vs event capacity, raising 23P01 which both routes map to 409. Crucially the trigger is EVENT-LEVEL ONLY — tier capacity has no DB or serialised enforcement (EV-3). Ticket lines are inserted after the booking row; the staff route rolls back on line-insert failure, the public route does not (EV-4).

Scheduling-side write validation is well-layered and shared: `validateEventCalendarPlacement` (venue hours + calendar working hours minus breaks via `validateExperienceEventWindowAgainstVenueAndCalendar`, staff leave via `findEventLeaveConflict`, then overlap scan via `assertExperienceEventWindowFreeOnCalendar` covering other events, calendar blocks, bookings and class instances) is used by both PATCH paths; POST inlines the same checks per date for range efficiency. Guards (`experience-event-guards.ts`) block delete/calendar-clear while upcoming active bookings exist — but only for the single target row, which the parent_event_id ON DELETE CASCADE defeats (EV-1).

Cancellation: admin-only POST /[id]/cancel deactivates the event then cascades `cancelStaffBookingWithNotify` per booking (group-aware, C11/C12-fixed partial refunds, comms in after()). Refunds are gated on the booking's `cancellation_deadline` — a guest-policy gate misapplied to venue-fault cancellation (EV-2). Guest self-cancel goes through /api/confirm with the same deadline, correctly there. Partial ticket refunds and guest self-move remain deferred (CDE review).

Parallel dormant subsystem: the cron /api/cron/materialize-event-sessions expands `unified_calendars.recurrence_rule` into `event_sessions` rows (recurrence_key idempotency), which unified_scheduling venues would book via the `event_session_id` branch of booking/create and list via `getEventClassSlots`. Nothing in the codebase, migrations, or seeds ever writes `recurrence_rule`, so the whole chain is unreachable today (EV-8); its capacity check is also outside the CDE trigger.

Engine duplication (asked): event-ticket-engine (486) and class-session-engine (593) are architectural twins — compute / fetch / fetchForRange / buildOfferingSummaries — but share the real logic already (venue-wide-blocks-fetch, venue-wide-business-hours, entity-booking-window, capacity-status, feature-flag resolve). The genuinely mergeable duplication is WITHIN event-ticket-engine: `fetchEventInput` should be `fetchEventInputForRange(date, date)` (EV-10). Cross-engine merging is not recommended: the two models carry deliberately different venue-gating rules (decision B: classes gated by explicit closures only, events must fit hours).

EventManagerView (2235 lines): one large but disciplined page component — pure helpers and analytics extracted to event-manager-utils.ts (tested), two extracted subcomponents, debounced relevance-filtered realtime refresh, and it is not a copy of ClassTimetableView (different structure and concerns). Its defects are behavioural (EV-7, EV-12), not structural.

**Quality assessment (finder agent):**

This domain is one of the better-disciplined corners of the codebase: the CDE-review remediation genuinely landed (shared validate-event-ticket-booking, upsert-by-id ticket sync, shared placement validator, DB capacity trigger with 409 mapping, fail-closed offerings reads), comments cite decisions and prior findings, and pure logic is extracted and tested. The layering — engine snapshot, shared route validator, DB trigger — is real defence in depth.

The remaining defects cluster where layers stop short of the edges: the delete guard checks one row while the schema cascades a whole series (EV-1); the refund gate reuses guest policy for venue-fault cancellation (EV-2); the trigger stops at event level, leaving tiers to a read-then-insert race (EV-3); and the public route's post-insert ticket-line write is the one unguarded copy of a guarded pattern (EV-4).

Over-building: the dormant event_sessions/unified-scheduling event pipeline (cron + booking branch + slot lister for a table nothing can populate) and the caller-less, schema-drifted [id] PATCH/DELETE are pure dead weight that actively confuses ("two event systems", "two PATCH paths") — roughly 500-600 removable lines. Under-building: series lifecycle. Creation materialises 104 rows happily, but there is no atomic create, no series edit, and no series cancel/delete semantics beyond an accidental cascade — the product edits and deletes occurrences one at a time while the schema and catalogue think in series. Efficiency is acceptable for venue-scale data, with two concrete regressions (doubled query in fetchEventInput; per-date sequential validation on series create) worth fixing.

### D7-resources

**Architecture summary (finder agent):**

Resources ("Model E": rooms, courts, equipment) are rows in `unified_calendars` with `calendar_type='resource'`. One row = one unit; there is no quantity column (src/types/booking-models.ts:448-492), so "3 identical courts" are 3 rows. Each resource must be hosted on a non-resource team calendar column via `display_on_calendar_id` (required at create, src/app/api/venue/resources/route.ts:373-378), and two resources may share a host column only if their weekly hours never overlap (route.ts:239-249) — a write-time guard backed at read time by engine-side "sibling exclusion".

The engine is src/lib/availability/resource-booking-engine.ts (1232 lines). Composition per date, in `buildResourceEngineInputFromParts` (:556-658): resource weekly hours or per-date `availability_exceptions` (:183-196), intersected with host calendar hours (:211-221), minus host appointment occupancy and sibling resource windows (:634-646, both subtractions — recorded in the Resolver Plan as legitimate re-anchoring occupancy), intersected with venue-wide business hours/closures. Host breaks, host leave and calendar blocks are per-candidate VETOES (:381-401), not subtractions, per operator decision (A) so the slot grid stays anchored. Slots step by `slot_interval_minutes` from each range start; requested duration is clamped into [min,max] (:359-362). `fetchResourceInput` (:1167) assembles one day; `prefetchResourceMonthForAvailability` (:716) batches a month; `attachHostCalendarsToResources` (:1098) enriches host hours/breaks plus, only when given a dateRange, leave and blocks via `fetchHostUnavailableWindows` (:1005).

Consumers: public day slots via /api/booking/availability (handleResourceAvailability, route.ts:798-828); public month picker /api/booking/resource-calendar; staff twins /api/venue/resource-availability and /api/venue/resource-calendar (both fail closed via withScheduleFailClosed, Stage 7); guest create /api/booking/create resource branch (:1538-1635) which re-runs the engine, checks `isGuestBookingDateAllowed` and `isResourceBookingStartInPast`, snapshots `resource_payment_requirement`, and prices `ceil(duration/interval) * price_per_slot_pence`. Modification is layered: shared dry-run `validateResourceBookingModification` (engine re-run with excludeBookingId + slot-interval-multiple rule), then an optimistic-concurrency UPDATE keyed on `updated_at`, used by staff PATCH /api/venue/bookings/[id] (:1857-1960) and guest self-reschedule in /api/confirm (:1034-1127, gated by the `guest_self_reschedule` flag). The final layer is the DB trigger `enforce_cde_capacity` (supabase/migrations/20261225120000_cde_capacity_guards.sql): advisory-lock-serialised half-open overlap check per resource, mapped to 409 — so resources, unlike appointments, DO have a concurrency guard.

The dashboard: /dashboard/resource-timeline (ResourceTimelineView.tsx, 2166 lines) is NOT a diary fork of PractitionerCalendarView — it is a CRUD settings page (list, multi-step form, per-day bookings list, delete guarded by upcoming-bookings check) that reuses the shared WeeklyHoursEditor and BookingDetailPanel. Resources render on the actual diary inside PractitionerCalendarView as mint "free slot" overlays via computeResourceAvailabilityMintSlots. Settings CRUD is /api/venue/resources (794 lines) with payment-config validation, managed-calendar permission scoping, and `assertResourceAvailabilityClearOnHostCalendar` (resource-host-calendar-conflicts.ts) blocking assignment when classes/events/bookings already occupy the host column during resource hours.

Payments are integrated: payment_requirement none/deposit/full_payment/card_hold on the row, deposits/full payment set status Pending awaiting payment, card_hold gated by a venue flag with degrade-to-none on both catalogue and create, auto-cancel cron covers all models, and cancellation_deadline is re-pinned on reschedule with the C13 no-resurrection rule. The waitlist is NOT integrated: waitlist_entries has 'table' and appointment kinds only — resources have no waitlist path (and the UI does not promise one).

Enforcement summary: hours/exceptions/host intersection — engine (all read paths); overlap — engine + DB trigger; booking windows — create route only (see findings); slot-grid alignment — UI + modify validator, NOT the create API; sibling exclusivity — write-time weekly check + engine subtraction; host leave/blocks — engine veto on day paths only (month paths drop it).

**Quality assessment (finder agent):**

This is the best-disciplined of the CDE models: a pure engine with a single fixturable seam, layered modification validation (dry-run, optimistic concurrency, DB advisory-lock trigger), deliberate documented veto-vs-subtract decisions, fail-closed wrappers on read routes, and write-time guards (sibling overlap, host schedule conflicts, delete blocked by upcoming bookings). The comments record operator decisions, which repeatedly saved this audit from false positives.

Where it is under-built: the guest-facing edges. The create API validates a clamped duration but stores the raw window (RS-1); minimum notice stops at midnight (RS-2); guest self-reschedule inherits staff semantics wholesale and so skips every guest booking-window rule (RS-3). The month picker was wired to the Stage 5 host-leave fix on the day path but not the month path (RS-4, RS-6). The pattern is consistent: the core engine is right, and the thin route/glue layers around it diverge from it.

Where it is over-built: the API surface is duplicated per audience (public/staff pairs for options, calendar, availability — six routes where three parameterised handlers would do), interval arithmetic exists in triplicate, and ~70 lines of schema-drift retry machinery persists for migrations a year old. ResourceTimelineView at 2166 lines is large but is genuine form/UX code reusing shared components, not a diary near-copy; it is acceptable. Multi-unit capacity (quantity > 1) is simply not modelled, and the one-resource-per-column exclusivity rule makes N identical units expensive to represent; that is a product ceiling worth a deliberate decision rather than an accident.

### D8-waitlist-comms

**Architecture summary (finder agent):**

**Appointment waitlist.** Guests join via POST /api/booking/appointment-waitlist (route.ts, 149 lines): admin-client insert into `waitlist_entries` (kind 'appointment', status 'waiting') after service validation, E.164 phone normalisation and a phone+date dedupe. A time preference is stored as desired_time/desired_time_end (all-day / exact / range, parsed by waitlist-time-window.ts).

The lifecycle is driven from three triggers. (1) Cancel hooks: staff-cancel-booking.ts:432, venue/bookings/[id]/route.ts:1136 and the guest confirm route (confirm/route.ts:845) all call offerAppointmentWaitlistOnCancel (387 lines). It gates on the `waitlist_v2` flag, loads waiting entries for the date, matches on service+practitioner+time window (findMatchingWaitlistEntries), then runs the availability engine on the freed slot (SA-H6 fix) before dispatching per venue mode from `feature_flags.waitlist_config`: `staff_choose` records a `waitlist_slot_opportunities` row (staff banner), `notify_all` notifies every match and terminally marks them 'confirmed', `notify_in_order` (default) offers the FIFO head a 30-minute offer (waitlist-offer-in-order.ts) after a four-query "slot already offered" guard. (2) A 5-minute cron (/api/cron/expire-waitlist-offers) expires offers past `expires_at` and cascades to the next waiting match (process-expired-waitlist-offers.ts), then re-derives staff_choose opportunities platform-wide (sync-staff-choose-waitlist-opportunities.ts → ensureStaffChooseOpportunitiesFromWaitlist, which runs the full availability engine per waiting entry). (3) Staff actions: PATCH /api/venue/waitlist status='offered' routes through manual-appointment-waitlist-offer.ts (prefers an open opportunity, else availability sampling); the alerts endpoint (venue/waitlist/alerts) offers-from-opportunity or dismisses.

Offers hold nothing: the slot stays publicly bookable, "whoever books first wins", so the only double-booking window is the known C3/SA-C1 race. The offer's 'confirmed' status plus expires_at gives the guest a `waitlist_offer` bypass consumed by availability/create/validate-appointment-slot routes (validate-waitlist-offer-access.ts): it only enables skipPastSlotFilter and conversion bookkeeping; availability is always fully re-checked at create (create/route.ts:1202-1219 with the C3-interim recheck). Offer expiry is cron-driven for cascade; requests independently treat past-expiry offers as invalid (read-only), so there is no double handling. Statuses are confusing: 'confirmed' means "guest was notified" (waitlist-offer-constants.ts), 'offered' is legacy/table-only.

**Communications.** One real pipeline: sendPolicyMessage (outbound.ts) → resolveCommPolicy (policy-resolver.ts; per-venue lane policies from `venues.communication_policies`, 60s cached in policies.ts, SMS gated by tier/quota/billing) → renderCommunicationEmail/Sms (renderer.ts, 985 lines, one switch over ~17 message keys, two lanes 'table'/'appointments_other') → deliverEmailMessage/deliverSmsMessage (delivery.ts). Delivery idempotency is a pending-row claim on communication_logs under a partial unique index (booking_id, message_type incl. channel suffix, lane), with 'dedupe' (send once, retry failed/stale-pending), 'upsert' (resend resets the same row; used for modification/cancellation/receipts) and 'guest_log' (CRM, guest-anchored) modes. Reschedules clear reminder log rows (scheduled-log-reset.ts) so crons re-send. Entry points: send-templated.ts wrappers (confirmation, deposit, card-hold, modification, cancellation, receipts), sendCustomBookingMessage/GuestMessage, owner-booking-notification (email, dedupe) and staff push (expo-push.ts fan-out with per-user prefs/quiet hours; unlogged, best-effort). A legacy facade (index.ts sendCommunication → service.ts CommunicationService) survives for auto-cancel/no-show/deposit-reminder crons and rebuilds context from ids before delegating to sendPolicyMessage; its channels/ classes are dead. Outside the pipeline, three senders call SendGrid/Twilio directly with no communication_logs row: appointment waitlist offers (send-appointment-waitlist-offer.ts, policy-checked), class-commerce receipts (send-class-commerce.ts, policy-checked, idempotency left to callers) and marketing contact sends.

Scheduled sends: /api/cron/send-communications (every 15 min) runs three near-identical table-lane loops in the route plus parameterized appointment/CDE lane runners (lib/cron/unified-scheduling-comms.ts). Timing is venue-timezone-correct (comms-timing.ts converts venue wall-clock to UTC, ±22-minute tolerance), closure-suppressed (SA-M2), and deduped via the log claim. delivery-health.ts reconciles "booking happened, guest contactable, zero rows logged". booking-log-email cron claims a per-schedule-key delivery row before sending. Templates render venue-local wall-clock dates/times safely; the one hardcoded zone is the cancellation-deadline formatter (Europe/London).

**Quality assessment (finder agent):**

The communications core is the best-disciplined code in this domain: a single policy→render→deliver pipeline with a genuine DB-level idempotency claim, per-channel log types, reschedule-aware dedupe resets, timezone-correct cron windows, and a delivery-health reconciler that audits its own blind spot. The appointment-lane cron runner is properly parameterized. The weakness is at the edges: three senders (waitlist offers, class commerce, marketing) bypass the logging/dedupe layer entirely, a dead legacy facade (channels classes, silent no-op message types) invites misuse, and the route-level table-lane crons re-implement 250 lines the lib already parameterizes.

The waitlist subsystem is over-fragmented (35+ files for one feature) but the decomposition is mostly real; the deeper problems are seams that were never finished: the offer-link builder, offer-bypass validation and conversion tracking form a complete layer that is unreachable because no message ever includes the offer parameter; a 437-line staff-booking converter is entirely dead; and enforcement that exists on one path (the SA-H6 availability gate on cancel-driven offers) is missing on its siblings (expiry cascade, opportunity-based manual offers). Concurrency is handled with compare-and-set status guards, which keep the database consistent, but notifications are consistently sent before the claim, so duplicates are prevented nowhere messages actually leave the building. Read paths are under-engineered for scale: the dashboard list and the 5-minute cron run the full availability engine per waiting entry with no pagination, date pruning, or entry expiry. Overall: sensible architecture, uneven follow-through; the bugs cluster where a second layer was assumed but never wired.

### D9-architecture

**Architecture summary (finder agent):**

Layering. Next.js App Router with 378 API route handlers under src/app/api, page-level client components under src/app/dashboard, and domain logic in src/lib (booking 18.3k lines, availability 9.6k, import 9.0k, linked-accounts 6.3k, communications 6.1k, compliance 5.7k). Supabase access is three clients (src/lib/supabase): browser anon, cookie-or-Bearer route client, and a service-role admin singleton. Venue-route auth is genuinely uniform: createVenueRouteClient + getVenueStaff (src/lib/venue-auth.ts, 479 lines, imported by 197 route files) resolves staff through the admin client with a 30s identity cache, then layered requireAdmin / managed-calendar checks. Zod validates bodies on 138 routes. The mobile "v1" API is thin delegation (re-exports of internal routes, plus self-HTTP fetches into /api/confirm for token cancel), with Bearer support in the shared route client, so there is no parallel mobile implementation. Cron routes uniformly use requireCronAuthorisation + withCronRunLogging.

Availability. Five engines under src/lib/availability, one per booking model. The table engine (engine.ts, 839; restaurant) is out of scope. The appointment engine (appointment-engine.ts, 1,860) computes per-practitioner slot grids with a sweep-line parallel-capacity check, and carries three validators (grid slots, exact starts for chains, custom intervals with explicit staff-override flags; full-day leave is checked ahead of every override). Class (593) and event (486) engines are capacity-count engines over fixed sessions, not grids. The resource engine (1,232) is a slot-grid engine with host-calendar projection, sibling exclusion, and month prefetch. A real shared semantic core already exists: venue-wide-business-hours.ts (the single resolver for weekly hours / closures / amended hours), venue-wide-blocks-fetch, calendar-hours (hours/breaks/days_off), entity-booking-window, capacity-status, venue-local-clock, and the fail-open-read instrumentation (availability-read-failure + AsyncLocalStorage collector in schedule-read-context + withScheduleFailClosed converting reported failures into guest-facing 503s), plus a parity harness (src/lib/availability/parity) that pins read/write agreement. Every fetcher issues Promise.all reads and substitutes [] on error - fail-open by design, made visible by the reporter.

Enforcement layers for a booking: engine slot list -> create-route revalidation (createAppointmentSlotRecheck / validateExactAppointmentStart / validateAppointmentCustomInterval) -> no DB exclusion constraint (C3/SA-C1, known and deferred). Write orchestration lives in three giant route files: booking/create (2,185 lines, guest, all five models), confirm (2,014 lines, anonymous HMAC manage/cancel/reschedule), venue/bookings/[id] (3,151 lines, staff PATCH). Their per-model logic mostly calls shared lib validators; the routes themselves are the branching glue.

Is a shared engine core worth it? Full unification is not: grid engines and capacity-count engines differ genuinely, and the scheduling audit's Phase 1 "one resolver" already targets the real semantic duplication (replace-vs-intersect amended hours). What remains is mechanical duplication: near-identical day/range fetcher pairs in class and event engines, a production-dead legacy practitioner path inside fetchAppointmentInput, card-hold degrade logic triplicated, and minute-range arithmetic (overlaps/intersect/subtract/timeToMinutes) re-implemented in 20+ places. The known "any available" per-practitioner refetch is SA-L16.

Types. There are NO generated Supabase Database types anywhere; all three clients are untyped SupabaseClient, so every row crosses the DB boundary via unchecked `as` casts (~397 sites). Offsetting that, only 12 `as any` exist in 423k non-test lines.

Tests. 378 vitest files (56k lines), concentrated in src/lib (310 files) and largely behavioural (parity matrix, and a meta-test that greps route sources to pin which routes are wrapped fail-closed). Route-level tests are thin (26 files, mostly card-hold aspects and crons). E2E is 4 Playwright specs (288 lines): appointment booking x3 and guest self-reschedule; nothing for classes, events, resources, or staff surfaces.

Migrations. 262 files, consistently named, no duplicate timestamp prefixes (some future-dated into 2027 as an ordering namespace); destructive SQL is confined to intentional merge/hard-delete functions.

**Quality assessment (finder agent):**

This is an unusually disciplined codebase for its size: one auth pattern actually adopted (197 routes), 12 `as any` in 423k lines, canonical shared resolvers with operator decisions recorded in comments, a parity test harness, and a meta-test that greps route source to stop fail-closed coverage regressing. The engineering culture visibly learned from the prior audits. The library layer (src/lib/availability, venue-wide-business-hours, entity-booking-window, processing-time) earns its keep; abstractions are rarely ceremonial - the few dead ones are small (requireRouteUser) or orphaned features (create-appointment-from-waitlist). Where it is under-built: (1) the DB boundary - no generated Supabase types, so ~397 casts are the only contract with the schema, a bad fit with deliberately fail-open reads; (2) the fail-closed mechanism instruments only the appointment engine's capacity reads, leaving the class/event/resource oversell reads invisible to it despite its own docs warning about exactly that; (3) e2e and route-level tests barely touch the three largest write orchestrators. Where it is over-built/over-grown: the 8,900-line practitioner calendar component and 5,900-line guest booking flow, the 2,550-line PATCH function, and mechanical copy-paste (fetcher day/range pairs, 23 timeToMinutes, verbatim range-arithmetic copies). The single change with the best defect-rate payoff is generating Database types and typing the three clients - it converts silent schema drift, today amplified by fail-open reads into wrong availability, into compile errors across all 397 cast sites. Second is decomposing venue/bookings/[id] PATCH into per-action handlers.
