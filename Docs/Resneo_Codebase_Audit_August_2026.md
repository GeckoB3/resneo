# ResNeo codebase audit, August 2026: code quality, correctness, and architecture

**Date:** 2026-08-22
**Code audited:** `main` at `491832c` ("Fix three booking-modification defects found in appointment test pass (#158)"), on branch `claude/resneo-codebase-audit-lu4gbf`.
**Method:** nine parallel deep-audit agents, one per domain, each followed by an adversarial verifier charged with refuting every claim by re-reading the cited code and hunting for a second enforcing layer the finder missed. The orchestrator additionally read the appointment engine and the largest components first-hand and independently re-verified the one Critical finding. Working state, raw agent output, and per-finding verdicts are in `Resneo_Codebase_Audit_August_2026_Worksheet.md` and `Resneo_Codebase_Audit_August_2026_Worksheet_RawResults.md`.
**Scope and priorities (operator instruction):** appointments first, then classes, events, resources. Restaurant/table/floor-plan features out of scope. Goals: understand how everything works; judge whether the code is sensible, disciplined and efficient; identify over-complex or excessive code where simpler would be better; identify bugs and gaps.
**Relationship to prior audits:** this audit deliberately does not re-litigate `Resneo_Forensic_Audit_August_2026.md` (security/RLS, C0-C13, D1) or `Resneo_Scheduling_Availability_Audit_August_2026.md` (scheduling semantics, SA-*). Findings that turned out to duplicate tracked items were struck by the verifiers and are listed in §4.5. Everything else here is new.

**Verification statement.** 141 raw findings were produced; every one was adversarially verified. 114 were CONFIRMED, 23 ADJUSTED (the core claim stands but a detail, severity or mechanism was corrected; the corrected version appears here), and 4 REFUTED (excluded; §4.5). The verifiers also surfaced 12 adjacent defects the finders missed; these are folded in below with a `+` suffix on the parent ID. Every anchor cited in §4 was resolved against the code at `491832c` by at least one verifying agent; anchors marked "orchestrator re-verified" were additionally checked by hand.

## 1. Verdict in brief

ResNeo is a substantially better-engineered codebase than its file sizes suggest, and materially better than when the May-August 2026 audits were written. The verdict splits cleanly along one line:

**The cores are genuinely disciplined.** The appointment availability engine is single-sourced, venue-local wall-clock throughout, DST-correct, half-open-interval correct at boundaries, and covered by an unusually good parity harness that pins read/write agreement across engines. The payments core (integer pence everywhere, conditional-UPDATE idempotency, a real webhook claim table, CAS-guarded card-hold charging, four layers of confirm redundancy) is the strongest domain in the repo. Class capacity, credit spend, and purchase fulfilment are enforced in the right layer (DB advisory-lock trigger, atomic locked RPC, unique-key fulfilment locks). Server create routes re-derive price, duration, deposit and slot validity from the catalogue and never trust the client. Auth is genuinely uniform (one pattern, 197 routes). There are 12 `as any` in 423k lines. Comments cite prior audit IDs and record operator decisions. This is a codebase that visibly learned from its audits.

**The edges are where the defects live, and the disease is copy-paste.** Almost every confirmed bug sits where a disciplined core was duplicated instead of shared, and the copies drifted: two staff-cancel implementations (one restores class credits, one silently burns them); four pasted refund blocks (two converge on `charge_already_refunded`, two fail the cancel); two visit-move implementations (the ad-hoc calendar one is strictly weaker than the endpoint built to replace it); day/month and day/range fetcher pairs that disagree; a "single source of truth" status list with private copies, one genuinely drifted; 23 definitions of `timeToMinutes`. The reverse paths (cancel, restore, refund, release) consistently lack the discipline of the forward paths (book, charge, consume). And roughly 3,000-4,000 lines are provably dead or unreachable in production yet still maintained.

**Is it over-complex?** Mostly no, in the sense that matters: there is very little speculative abstraction, and most length is earned by real race handling and real business rules. The complexity problem is almost entirely *duplication* and *monolith accretion* (an 8,903-line calendar component, a 5,903-line booking flow, a 2,556-line PATCH function, a 2,014-line seven-flow route). The correct programme is consolidation and deletion, not redesign. §5 sizes it: roughly 6,000-7,500 lines can be deleted or collapsed with no behaviour change, and doing so removes the drift surface that produced most of the bugs in §4.

**Headline defects** (full detail in §4): one Critical (deleting an event-series parent silently cascade-deletes every occurrence in the series and orphans their sold bookings); staff dashboard cancels permanently burn class credits and membership allowances; a venue cancelling its own event inside the guest's notice window keeps every guest's money and blames the guest's timing in the email; cash-settled deposits make bookings permanently uncancellable in-product; the entire appointment waitlist offer-link layer is unreachable (offers link to the bare booking page, where the offered slot is hidden); resource guest-create stores a raw 14-hour window it only validated 3 hours of; and ticket-tier capacity plus unified group sessions are the two capacity models with no guard at any layer.

## 2. How ResNeo works

This section is the architecture map the findings hang off. It reflects what the code actually does at `491832c`, verified by agents that read it, not the design docs.

### 2.1 The shape of the system

Next.js 16 App Router + Supabase (Postgres, 262 migrations) + Stripe. ~423k lines of TypeScript in ~2,000 files. Three Supabase clients (`src/lib/supabase`): browser anon, cookie-or-Bearer route client, service-role admin singleton; none is typed with a generated `Database` type (§4, AR-2). Domain logic lives in `src/lib` (booking 18.3k lines, availability 9.6k, import 9.0k, communications 6.1k); route handlers under `src/app/api` (378 of them) are mostly branching glue over lib validators, except for three giant write orchestrators (`booking/create` 2,185 lines; `confirm` 2,014; `venue/bookings/[id]` 3,151). Venue-route auth is uniform: `createVenueRouteClient` + `getVenueStaff` (imported by 197 route files) with layered admin/managed-calendar checks. The mobile "v1" API is thin delegation over the internal routes, not a parallel implementation. Cron routes uniformly use `requireCronAuthorisation` + `withCronRunLogging`.

There are five booking models sharing one `bookings` table: table reservation (out of scope), appointments (Model B), classes (C), events/tickets (D), resources (E), plus a sixth dormant one (unified `event_sessions`, §4 EV-8). Each model has its own availability engine under `src/lib/availability`; a shared semantic core already exists and is genuinely shared: `venue-wide-business-hours.ts` (the single resolver for weekly hours, closures, amended hours), `calendar-hours.ts` (calendar hours/breaks/days-off), `entity-booking-window.ts`, `capacity-status.ts`, `venue-local-clock.ts`, and the fail-open-read instrumentation (`availability-read-failure` -> AsyncLocalStorage collector -> `withScheduleFailClosed`, which converts a known-incomplete 200 into a guest-facing 503, but only for reads that report).

**Enforcement layering for a booking** (the pattern the whole system relies on): engine slot list -> create-route re-validation -> DB guard. The DB guard is `enforce_cde_capacity` (migration `20261225120000`): an advisory-lock-serialised BEFORE INSERT/UPDATE trigger covering resource overlap, event-level capacity and class capacity. Appointments have no DB-level guard (the known, deferred C3); ticket tiers and `event_sessions` have none either (new findings EV-3, PB-1).

### 2.2 Appointment availability (the engine)

`appointment-engine.ts` (1,860 lines) holds three entry points over one rule set: `computeAppointmentAvailability` (slot generation), `validateExactAppointmentStart` (exact-time write gate for multi-service chains; fixed start times still enforced), and `validateAppointmentCustomInterval` (staff move/resize/walk-in, with per-gate override flags `allowOutsideHours` / `allowDuringBreaks` / `allowInsideNoticeWindow` / `allowBookingOverlap`; full-day leave is checked ahead of every override, per SA-M3). All arithmetic is venue-local wall-clock minutes on half-open intervals; UTC conversion lives only in `venue-local-clock.ts` (two-pass offset resolver, DST-correct, test-pinned). Minimum notice is deliberately wall-clock (`slotMinutesFromNow`) so the rule is stable across DST and works across dates. Closures and breaks are **vetoed per candidate rather than subtracted** from the anchoring ranges, so a part-day closure never re-anchors the afternoon slot grid; candidate starts come from `booking-interval.ts` (interval grid, hour-anchored minute marks, or fixed times). Processing time produces split busy intervals (`practitionerBusyMinuteOffsets`) and `peakConcurrentBookings` counts true peak concurrency against `parallel_clients` (a candidate spanning two back-to-back bookings counts 1x2, not 2). Boundary behaviour at day edges, buffers and variants is correct in everything traced. The prior audit's "two resolvers" programme has substantially shipped: the parity harness (`parity/scheduling-world.ts` + 6 test files) drives one fixture through every engine and pins each old divergence as CONVERGED.

The **fetch layer** below the engine is where discipline decays: four near-copy input builders (day/month x legacy/unified) plus a fifth in `appointment-catalog.ts`, where two would do; the legacy halves are production-dead (zero `practitioners` rows, verified by query in the scheduling audit); the month copy has drifted from the day copy (§4 AE-2). Reads fail open by design and are instrumented, but a handful of reads bypass the instrumentation (AE-1, AR-1).

**API surface:** `/api/booking/availability` is the live guest day route (variants, add-ons, custom durations, any-available pooling); `/api/booking/appointment-calendar` is the month picker; `/api/booking/validate-appointment-slot` the exact-start gate; `/api/booking/unified-availability` a second, first-party-consumerless public day route (AE-4). The staff booking surface deliberately consumes the public day route (a documented design decision in `booking-flow-api.ts`), which is decision-relevant for any route consolidation.

### 2.3 Public appointment booking (guest write path)

Client: `AppointmentBookingFlow.tsx` (5,903 lines; 48 useState, 20 useEffect, ~30 step values) is a hand-rolled step machine; the step-ordering logic was extracted to a tested pure module (`appointment-flow-order.ts`), but per-step JSX, state and fetching remain monolithic, and the group flow duplicates the single flow's service/variant/add-on/practitioner/slot steps nearly verbatim (PB-5). Server: three create routes. `/api/booking/create` (rate-limited, zod-validated) dispatches on venue mode to a per-model handler; `create-multi-service` (one guest, consecutive services) and `create-group` (many people) are ~70-75% line-identical copies of each other (PB-3), and neither is rate-limited (PB-7). The strong property, verified: **every branch re-fetches the engine input, re-validates the slot, and re-derives price/deposit/card-hold from the catalogue; client amounts are never trusted.** The appointment race is narrowed (not closed) by a re-validation immediately before insert (`revalidate-appointment-slot.ts`, the C3 interim). Combined-collective bookings are resolved server-side and re-attributed to the owning venue. Confirmation comms fire via `after()` for no-payment bookings; paid bookings notify from the payment-confirm path.

### 2.4 Staff appointment surfaces

Three surfaces over one API family. `PractitionerCalendarView.tsx` (8,903 lines; the component itself spans 2,483-8,903) renders the diary and owns six separable subsystems in one closure: data layer (32 fetch sites, 2 realtime channels), gesture engine (drag/resize/press-and-hold), a client-side visit orchestration that duplicates the `/visits` endpoints, an undo + deferred-guest-notify system, optimistic quick-patch with an overlay store, and ~2,300 lines of render (SA2-2). `DaySheetView` and the bookings dashboards converge by debounced refetch over realtime. The API: POST `/api/venue/bookings` (2,046 lines, five create branches; the appointment branch runs the same engine as public create, with walk-ins deliberately allowed past hours/notice but still checked against leave and scheduled classes); PATCH `/api/venue/bookings/[id]` is one 2,556-line function dispatching ~14 operations by sniffing body keys (SA2-8). Every appointment reschedule funnels through the shared `validateAppointmentModificationInterval`, the same engine path as availability, with explicit staff override flags mirrored by a dry-run route. Optimistic concurrency is `.eq('updated_at', prev)` -> 412. Multi-service visits are N rows sharing `group_booking_id`, made first-class by `appointment-visit.ts` and two `/visits/[gid]` endpoints that plan, dry-run, write row-by-row and roll back written rows on failure; the calendar's drag path does not use them (SA2-3/SA2-4). Cancellation exists twice (route PATCH branch vs `staff-cancel-booking.ts`) and the divergences between the copies are where the worst staff-side defects live (SA2-1/CL-1, SA2-7).

### 2.5 Payments, deposits, card holds

All money is integer pence end-to-end; floats exist only at display/engine boundaries with `Math.round(x*100)` conversion. Deposit lifecycle: one PaymentIntent per capture unit (booking, visit/group, or class cart); confirmation is quadruple-redundant (client confirm route -> webhook -> two self-heal sweeps -> daily reconciliation), all funnelling into one DB-only helper with conditional writes so replays are no-ops. Webhook idempotency is a claim table with stale-claim reclaim. Card holds (`booking_card_holds`, three capture modes) have a genuinely race-hardened charge engine (CAS claim, attempt-numbered idempotency keys, orphan-PI disposal) and versioned consent snapshots. Refunds since C11 are per-member shares of a shared PI; the plan->refund->stamp block is pasted at four sites and two lack the already-refunded convergence (PM-2). Crons (`auto-cancel-bookings` five sweeps, `deposit-reminder-2h`, `release-card-holds`, `reconciliation`, `subscription-reconcile`) verify with Stripe before acting and only act on rows their conditional UPDATE flipped, but trust small datasets: unbounded or 200-capped serial loops, no `maxDuration`, no alert dedupe (PM-9, PM-11, PM-12). `/api/confirm` is not the payment confirm: it is the guest manage-link handler (GET assembler + confirm + cancel + modify with four model branches sharing a 4x-pasted scaffold, PM-5). The code matches `CARD_HOLD_DEPOSITS_DESIGN_AND_IMPLEMENTATION.md` closely where checked.

### 2.6 Classes

Four layers. **Availability:** `class-session-engine.ts` computes remaining capacity per materialised `class_instances` row (explicit closures gate scheduled classes, not the weekly baseline, per operator decision); capacity statuses exactly match the DB trigger. **Booking writes:** two near-copy insert helpers (free vs pending-paid); the authoritative race guard is the `enforce_cde_capacity` advisory-lock trigger, which is what makes the app-level pre-checks safe. **Commerce** (`class-commerce`, ~4.7k lines): `quoteClassCart` prices lines and applies the best membership discount; `orchestrateClassCartCheckout` re-quotes server-side and resolves entitlement per line with precedence free -> course -> membership -> credits(opt-in) -> card; credits spend through an atomic Postgres RPC (advisory lock, FIFO by expiry, idempotent per booking); paid lines share one PI; failures trigger compensating rollback. **Fulfilment/lifecycle:** the Stripe webhook routes PI purposes to credit-purchase and course-enrollment fulfillers (unique fulfilment locks + compensation) and membership sync (upsert on `stripe_subscription_id`). Five crons handle recurring materialisation, membership period reset, credit expiry, membership reconcile, and pending-course expiry. The forward paths are excellent; the *reverse* paths (restore credits/allowance on cancel) never received the same discipline and hold most of the class findings: restores are non-atomic JS read-modify-write, the main staff cancel path restores nothing, the expiry cron claws back restored credits, and `materialize-recurring-reservation.ts` is 454 lines of untested date logic with two real defects (CL-1..CL-8).

### 2.7 Events

A materialised-occurrence model: every occurrence is its own `experience_events` row with child `event_ticket_types`; recurrence is expanded at create time (up to 104 rows), the first occurrence is designated series parent and siblings point `parent_event_id` at it. There is no series-level edit, no atomic series create, and no deliberate series delete; the schema's `ON DELETE CASCADE` on `parent_event_id` supplies an accidental one (EV-1, the audit's only Critical). Booking enforcement is three-layered (engine snapshot -> shared `validateEventTicketBooking` with server-derived prices -> event-level DB trigger), but the trigger stops at event totals: tier capacity is app-only (EV-3), and the public route's ticket-line insert is the one unguarded copy of a guarded pattern (EV-4). Venue-initiated event cancellation reuses the guest-policy refund gate, so a late venue cancellation keeps guests' money (EV-2). A parallel dormant subsystem (cron-materialised `event_sessions` on unified calendars) is unreachable: nothing ever writes `unified_calendars.recurrence_rule` (EV-8), yet its booking branch also carries the only other capacity model with no DB guard (PB-1). `EventManagerView` (2,235 lines) is large but disciplined; its defects are behavioural, not structural.

### 2.8 Resources

Rooms/courts/equipment are `unified_calendars` rows with `calendar_type='resource'`, one row per unit (quantity is not modelled), hosted on a team calendar column with a write-time sibling-overlap guard and engine-side sibling exclusion. The engine (1,232 lines) composes per date: resource hours (or per-date exceptions) ∩ host hours, minus host appointment occupancy and sibling windows (subtractions, recorded as legitimate re-anchoring occupancy), ∩ venue-wide hours; host breaks/leave/blocks are per-candidate vetoes so the grid stays anchored. Modification is layered (shared dry-run validator -> optimistic concurrency -> the DB overlap trigger), so resources, unlike appointments, do have a real concurrency guard. This is the best-disciplined CDE model at the core; its defects are all in the thin route/glue layers around it: create stores what it did not validate (RS-1), notice stops at midnight (RS-2), guest self-reschedule inherits staff semantics wholesale (RS-3), and the month routes forgot the date range that loads host leave (RS-4). `ResourceTimelineView` (2,166 lines) is genuine settings/form code reusing shared components, not a diary fork.

### 2.9 Waitlist and communications

**Waitlist** (appointments): guests join via a public route; the lifecycle is driven from cancel hooks (all three cancel paths call `offerAppointmentWaitlistOnCancel`, which availability-gates the freed slot per SA-H6 and dispatches per venue mode: staff_choose opportunity rows, notify_all, or FIFO `notify_in_order` with a 30-minute offer), a 5-minute cron that expires offers and cascades to the next guest, and staff actions. Offers hold nothing; the slot stays publicly bookable by design. The subsystem is over-fragmented (35+ files) and its deepest defect is a seam never finished: the offer-URL builder, offer-bypass validation and conversion tracking form a complete, tested layer that is unreachable because no message ever includes the offer parameter (WC-1). Enforcement that exists on the cancel path (the availability gate) is missing on its siblings (cascade WC-2, manual offers WC-7), and notifications are consistently sent *before* the status compare-and-set, so the DB stays consistent but guests can be double-messaged (WC-4).

**Communications:** one real pipeline (`sendPolicyMessage` -> per-venue lane policies -> renderer -> delivery) with genuine DB-level idempotency (a pending-row claim on `communication_logs` under a partial unique index, with dedupe/upsert/guest_log modes), reschedule-aware reminder resets, venue-timezone-correct cron windows, closure suppression, and a delivery-health reconciler. The weaknesses are at the edges: three senders (waitlist offers, class-commerce receipts, marketing) bypass the logging/dedupe layer entirely (WC-12); a dead legacy facade silently no-ops 9 of its 15 documented message types (WC-20); and the cron route re-implements the parameterised lane runner three times for the table lane (WC-13).

## 3. Systemic patterns

Seven patterns explain nearly every finding in §4. Fixing instances without fixing the pattern will regenerate the defects.

**P1. Forward paths are disciplined; reverse paths are not.** Booking, charging and consuming are guarded by triggers, locked RPCs, CAS claims and re-validation. Cancelling, restoring and refunding are hand-rolled, duplicated and drifted: staff cancel burns class credits (CL-1); restore is non-atomic while consume is locked (CL-5, CL-6); the expiry cron claws back restored credits (CL-4); two of four refund blocks lack the already-refunded convergence (PM-2); event cancel keeps guests' money (EV-2); checkout rollback deletes bookings before confirming the restore landed (CL-12). *Pattern fix: one cancel/restore implementation per concern, built to the same standard as its forward twin (idempotency key first, relative updates, observable failure).*

**P2. Copy-paste is the dominant defect engine.** Verified drift between copies, not hypothetical: `create-multi-service` vs `create-group` (~740-800 identical lines, compliance gate already written two ways); the create route's payment machinery pasted twice with the Stripe cleanup fix landing in only one copy (PB-4, drift already materialised); two staff cancels (SA2-1/SA2-7); two visit-move implementations (SA2-4); four refund blocks (PM-2); four `/api/confirm` modify scaffolds with the CDE_CAPACITY mapping in only two (PM-5); day/range fetcher pairs in class and event engines (AR-6); the month appointment fetcher diverged from the day one (AE-2); charge-formula variants across four class files letting deposit-only classes book free (CL-14); `subtractOneRange` byte-identical in three files, `timeToMinutes` defined 23 times, `overlaps` four times (AR-9); status lists with a drifted copy that breaks waitlist freed-slot detection (AE-7). *Pattern fix: §5 Phase 1; extraction, then deletion.*

**P3. Monolith accretion.** `PractitionerCalendarView.tsx` 8,903 lines (76 useState), `AppointmentBookingFlow.tsx` 5,903, PATCH `/api/venue/bookings/[id]` one 2,556-line key-sniffing function, `/api/confirm` 2,014 lines interleaving seven flows. Prior Criticals (C8, C9, C10) all lived in these files; size is why review misses gates. *Pattern fix: mechanical extraction with no behaviour change (§5 Phase 2).*

**P4. Enforcement layers stop one step short of the edges.** The layering principle (engine -> route re-check -> DB guard) is real but incomplete at exactly four seams: ticket tiers (EV-3), unified group sessions (PB-1), the fail-closed instrumentation (AE-1/AR-1: class/event/resource capacity reads and three scheduled-session reads report nothing, so the 503 wrapper is inert for them; mitigated for final oversell by the DB trigger except for tiers), and guest-facing glue around the resource engine (RS-1/RS-2/RS-3).

**P5. The DB boundary is unchecked.** No generated Supabase types anywhere; all three clients untyped; ~400-625 `as` casts are the only schema contract, and the deliberate fail-open/default-substitution read style converts silent schema drift into silently wrong availability instead of errors (AR-2). This is the single structural change with the widest defect-prevention reach.

**P6. Dead and unreachable code is retained and maintained.** ~600 lines of legacy practitioner fetch paths plus a routing round-trip on every request (AE-3); a ~750-line legacy mirror in the appointment-services route (SA2-9); a consumerless second public day-slot route (AE-4); the entire dormant `event_sessions` pipeline (EV-8, ~350 lines + a daily no-op cron); a dead 437-line waitlist booking module (WC-11); dead comms channel classes and silently no-op facade types (WC-20); dead email templates shown to owners in the gallery (WC-14); unreachable course proration (CL-18); a dead refund_message branch (PM-10). Dead code here is not inert: it drifts, it confuses ("two event systems"), and twice it sat one flag away from running with stale rules.

**P7. Test posture inverts the risk profile.** The lib layer has 310 behavioural test files including the parity harness and a fail-closed source-grep meta-test; the three giant write orchestrators that produced most historical Criticals have essentially no route-level tests, and e2e covers only appointment paths (4 specs, 288 lines): nothing for classes, events, resources, staff surfaces, or cancellation/refund flows (AR-12). `materialize-recurring-reservation.ts` (454 lines of date logic, two real bugs) has zero tests.

IDs are stable for cross-reference into the worksheet appendix, which carries the full evidence chains. ### 4.1 Critical

**EV-1 (bug) Deleting an event-series parent cascade-deletes every occurrence in the series and orphans their sold bookings.**
`supabase/migrations/20260327000001_multi_model_foundation.sql:100` (orchestrator re-verified).
`parent_event_id uuid REFERENCES experience_events(id) ON DELETE CASCADE`, never altered by any later migration. Since the C8 fix, every multi-date create designates the first (earliest) occurrence as series parent and points all siblings at it (`experience-events/route.ts:316-321, 441`). Both DELETE routes guard only the single target row: `assertExperienceEventDeletable` counts bookings `.eq('experience_event_id', id)` with `booking_date >= today` (`experience-event-guards.ts:12-31`), so siblings' bookings are never checked, and past bookings on the parent never block. Deleting the parent cascades to all sibling rows, cascades their `event_ticket_types`, and SET-NULLs their bookings' `experience_event_id`. The UI delete confirm (`EventManagerView.tsx:829-835`) gives no series warning. *Failure:* venue tidies up the week-1 card of a 20-week series after it has run; all 19 future occurrences vanish from sale and the calendar; their confirmed, often prepaid bookings stay `Booked` but lose their event link, disappearing from rosters and capacity counts; no guest is cancelled, refunded or notified. *Fix:* change the FK to `ON DELETE RESTRICT` (or SET NULL) **and** extend the delete guard to count bookings on rows with `parent_event_id = id`, offering an explicit series-cancel instead; add a "this deletes N future dates" confirmation for parents. Related: both PATCH schemas accept an arbitrary, un-venue-scoped `parent_event_id` (EV-9+ below), which makes the cascade reachable across venues; venue-scope it in the same change.

### 4.2 High

#### Appointments: availability and public booking

**AE-1 (gap) Scheduled-session block reads fail open via `console.warn`, invisible to the Stage 7 fail-closed wrapper.** `src/lib/availability/calendar-session-blocks.ts:53,89,125`. All three fetchers swallow errors with `console.warn(...); return []` instead of `reportAvailabilityReadFailure`, and they run on every guest day request (engine `:1417`, `:1820`); the wrapper converts only *reported* failures into 503s (its own doc says so). Two sibling silent reads compound it: the `service_items` read in `fetchCalendarAppointmentInput` discards its error entirely (`appointment-engine.ts:1530-1536`), and `appointment-catalog.ts:176-178` warns only. The month twin *does* report, so day and month behave differently for the identical failure. Because appointments have no DB guard (C3 deferred), the create-side re-check fails open the same way: a transient `class_instances` error can let a guest book an appointment on top of a scheduled class, with nothing in Sentry. *Fix:* route these reads (and the catalog one at `appointment-catalog.ts:93`) through the reporter; the wrapper then covers them with no other change. Do **not** sweep `blocked-range-models.ts:93-96` into the same fix: it fails open in the safe direction (all block sources ON) and converting it would wrongly 503.

**PB-1 (bug) Unified group-session (`event_session_id`) capacity can be oversold: no DB guard, no slot recheck.** `src/app/api/booking/create/route.ts:966`. The branch reads booked capacity (`:966-979`) and inserts ~900 lines later (`:1872`) with no recheck (the recheck is armed only in the appointment branch), and `enforce_cde_capacity` never inspects `event_session_id`. Two concurrent purchases of the last seat both pass and both insert. Today this is latent (the `event_sessions` pipeline is dormant, EV-8) but it is the one capacity model with no guard at any layer, and the branch is live code on a public route. Also (PB-1+): the guest-supplied `capacity_used` (schema allows up to 50) multiplies the deposit charge for sessions. *Fix:* either delete the branch with the dormant pipeline (EV-8), or extend the trigger with an `event_session_id` arm and map its 23P01 to 409.

**PB-3 (excess-code) `create-multi-service` and `create-group` are ~70-75% line-identical (~740-800 shared lines) and already diverging.** `create-multi-service/route.ts:125` / `create-group/route.ts`. Verified by diff; the compliance gate is already written two different ways in the two files. Every future fix (a new Stripe mode, a compliance change) must land twice or the routes drift. *Fix:* extract one `createVisit({entries, validateEntry: exact|grid, samePractitioner})` module; two ~120-line entry points remain.

#### Staff appointment surfaces

**CL-1 / SA2-1 (bug) Staff dashboard cancel never restores class credits or membership allowance; the UI steers staff into exactly this path.** `src/app/api/venue/bookings/[id]/route.ts:911-1224`. The PATCH `Cancelled` branch refunds Stripe deposits, settles card holds, offers waitlists and sends comms, but never calls `restoreClassCreditsForBooking` / `restoreMembershipAllowanceForBooking`. ## 7. Method and confidence

Nine finder agents (availability engine; public booking; staff surfaces; payments; classes; events; resources; waitlist/comms; cross-cutting architecture) each read their domain's code directly and were required to anchor every claim to a file:line read this session. Nine adversarial verifiers then attempted to refute every finding, specifically hunting for second enforcing layers (the prior audits' false positives all came from missing one) and checking claims against the tracked C*/SA-* registers. Outcomes: 114/141 confirmed, 23 adjusted, 4 refuted; 12 additional defects surfaced during refutation. The orchestrator read `appointment-engine.ts` in full, structurally mapped the two client monoliths and the create route, independently confirmed the 23-definition `timeToMinutes` count, and hand-re-verified EV-1 (FK, guard, both routes).

Confidence calibration: findings here are code-level claims verified by at least one independent re-read, not runtime reproductions; nothing was executed against a live database. Where a claim depended on production data shape (legacy-table emptiness, flag exposure), the audit relied on the prior audits' recorded production queries and says so inline. The three findings most deserving of a runtime confirmation before large remediation investment: CL-2 (cadence interaction with the cron schedule), PM-6 (Stripe consent/chargeback posture), and WC-4 (cron overlap frequency).
